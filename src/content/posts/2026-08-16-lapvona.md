**Rating: 3.5/5**

![cover art](https://cdn.thestorygraph.com/otpw9q7wv6f8plxtwfrg05167do0)

Wow, what a wild novel.

So crazy and yet so believable. Like a medieval dark fable written through a psychoanalytical lens. Grotesque, but isn't life grotesque anyway? There is this omniscient narrator that is making us watch all the events evolve in such a natural way that is scarily accurate. Shit happens and you're like, oh well, no wonder. While at the same time we get to see all the characters one by one, and we dive inside their heads one by one and we can observe their inner thinking, and how they perceive these events from their limited perspective; limited both by their actual position in space/time and by their superstitious view of reality. Both are lacks of knowledge in their own way. We can really see religion as a human need, that is all made up by these human fears and false beliefs but it will still strongly drive all characters' lives. It feels all so pathetic, tragic, and hilarious at the same time.

And then there is nature constantly in the background that just doesn't care at all, it just flows and goes on with its own business. But the few that can harness its mysteries are the real powerful characters, the famous witches and wizards, the heretics, in the eyes of the christian mind, and I would say of the modern mind as well, even to modern "atheists" of sorts. As I believe a skewed and dogmatic view of science has replaced the bigotry of religion with its own new bigotry of scientism.

A brilliant novel, with such a compelling and clear prose that makes it hard to put down.

Not for the weak at heart, for sure, but if you've got the stomach this is so good.
