**Rating: 1.5/5**

![cover art](https://cdn.thestorygraph.com/2ewc8pmg0qmi4aqgbibfcgo99rzc)

I swear this is the last time I fall for the internet hype, literally the worst read of the year for me. How this novel has won the Stoker prize is beyond me.

Clearly written in two different phases, with a filler story in the middle that spanned for way too long and it was comical in its execution.

Paper thin characters, videogame-like imagery, lack of real depth, overall I found it extremely boring.
The take on classicism through the usual Hollywood-level of depth, and the biblical elements didn't help at all, to be honest. At most the book conjured a Dark Souls-like sort of atmosphere, which I reckon could appeal to some, but not to me, especially in a novel.

The "modern" part of the book I guess was good enough, and I think it would have been better as a short story in itself.
The 1800 section, again, felt like an unnecessary and over drawn filler that was poorly written, a real slog!
I'm afraid that paraphrasing Moby Dick in the opening of your book is not enough to make it good.

In the note at the end the author says that it was hard to find a publisher for the novel because most genre publishers thought it was too literary. What??? You wish it was! Especially the middle section, once again, felt like fan-fiction at best!

And they say Lovecraft was a bad writer. Have a look over here, folks. To give you an example, the narrative device of the story-within-a-story is well established in literature, particularly in gothic/weird fiction, as a way to leverage the reader's suspension of belief as much as it is needed for the genre. Notably, there is a particularly famous case of extreme Russian dolls narrative structure in Lovecraft's most famous work "The Call of Chtulhu". In that case though, the resulting effect is convincing, as it conveys sort of a spiraling down into the abyss of reality until the final revelation. It's well done and with intent.

It seemed to me that Langan mimicked this narrative device for the sake of it, resulting derivative and ultimately boring.

I'm appalled by the low level of expectations of the average "modern reader".
I don't wanna sound like some kind of snob, which I don't consider myself in the slightest, but holy cow the bs that is printed out there is unfathomable.

Next time I should quit a book that I'm not liking this much, so this one is on me I guess...
