**Rating: 4/5**

![cover art](https://cdn.thestorygraph.com/ps2m2b607b1gilvjtq8ym3t2jei2)

This was my second attempt, the first time I tried to read Moby Dick was about 15 years ago, but in Italian, and I quit. This time I read it in English and it took me about 4 months. Kudos to me, I guess.

I ended up reading a chapter or two in bed every night. The prose tore me down, it was very hard, but I slowly digested it.

In the meantime I read about 10 other books, some of which were quite dense philosophy books (to me at least), like Plato's Republic, Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics, and a volume on the history of ancient philosophy.
Moby Dick was a tougher read, hands down. This is not an adventure story. This is like the bible of the seven seas.

I can see why it's a classic novel, although I didn't love it. It was an endless chore most of the time. Some chapters where brilliant though, poetic, potent, epic.

I can see also how a re-read would be rewarding. Now that I know the whole structure, I'd probably appreciate the details more.

But for now I'm good, thanks.

EDIT:

It's been not even a week since I finished reading Moby Dick. Despise my mixed-feelings review, I'm experiencing a weird longing for the book, as if it's a mystery that I haven't cracked somehow, as if it was trying to tell me something beyond the words, and I haven't quite caught it. I feel like re-reading it already.

This grants another star to my rating, I can see how powerful this novel is.
