**Rating: 4/5**

![cover art](https://cdn.thestorygraph.com/8jbjfv4kzpkyj96n3tezw4q2s553)

I had this book on my shelves for a few years, I'm glad I finally read it.

Once I started it I just couldn't put it down. It's written in a way that is relentless, gives you no breather and no pause.

It's bleak, and gut wrenching, devastating. It made me literally cry at the end.

I have this tendency to try and find some reasoning behind an author's work, his motivation for writing it; and I'm tempted to think of this book in terms of a warning against a nuclear war and its aftermath. But I think the settings of the book were just an excuse for McCarthy to show something about the human nature and existence. To show what happens when you've got nothing left, society has collapsed and you're just surviving. But most of all what happens in these circumstances if you have a kid with you. That gives you hope and a meaning maybe, maybe not.

<summary>"Light" spoiler about the end:</summary>

I'm really glad about the somewhat hopeful ending, I was bracing myself for some nihilistic crash, but it went well. All considered.
