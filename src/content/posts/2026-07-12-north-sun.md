**Rating: 3/5**

![cover art](https://cdn.thestorygraph.com/ooml8s1agipidzn29i8xehowrggq)

Mixed feelings on this one. I liked the prose, I liked the settings, especially a month after finishing Moby Dick. In fact I read it because I was craving some more Moby Dick, and in this sense North Sun somewhat delivered.

I don't shy away from fantastic elements, allegories, and speculative fiction at all, but in the case of this novel I felt too much was left open to the reader's interpretation.

<details>
<summary>Spoilers ahead:</summary>

What did really happened? Who was Old Sorrel? And Thule? And Leander? What was the golden egg and what does it stand for? No answers at all to all this.

</details>

It was overall an interesting read, very well written that leaned a bit too much on the fantastical elements without a coherent theory to somehow justify said elements.

<details>
<summary>More spoilers:</summary>

I could imagine Old Sorrel and everything else that is seen through the boys' eyes as a response to the trauma of being abused by Eastman, but all the rest of it, the occult aspects of the Ashleys family, the symbolic meaning of the golden egg heirloom, felt a bit too much as something that as a reader I just had to accept without too much questioning.

</details>

Overall a bit underwhelming and anti-climatic, but again, I loved its prose.
